Title: Cut the spec writer's and critic's turn count: batch reads, read line ranges, keep long output in scratch; the critic runs no test suites and builds no prototypes

Summary:
The spec writer is the most expensive agent role in the factory. It is the role that turns an accepted ticket into a spec, and its cost comes from how many turns it takes, because every turn re-sends the start-up context plus everything read so far. The requester wants prompt-only rules that cut those turns. The writer batches independent reads and commands into one turn, reads line ranges once `grep` has found them, writes long command output to its run's scratch directory and greps it, and drafts the spec in as few writes as possible. The critic is the role that reviews a spec before the operator approves it. It runs no test suites and builds no prototype clones (disposable checkouts with the change applied), and grounds each claim with at most two paths and one command; a claim that needs a build becomes a finding or a question. Nothing else changes: no rubric item, round limit or required spec section moves, and the operator compares old-prompt and new-prompt specs from 2–3 replayed intakes before the runtime moves.
